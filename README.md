## Case Study Report: Does the Journey Impact the Result
## LPGA 2022 Player Statistics and Performance Metrics
## Introduction:
Golf is a very unique sport. It lives at the intersection of precise skill and mental strength. Every player has their own opinion on what differentiates the best players from the others. Some say it is about their strength and abilitiy to hit long drives. Some say that it is all about what you do on the green. Others say that what is most important is keeping your ball on the fairway. Lastly, some live by staying out of the bunkers. There are many strategies that people invoke when describing how to win in golf, but is there a right one? Does the way you get to the score really matter? Are there some shots to absolutely avoid? This study aims to analyze LPGA 2022 player statistics to determine which performance metrics contribute most to winning tournaments and increasing earnings.

## Background:
The LPGA 2022 tournament data provides insights into player performance through various statistics, including greens in regulation, sand save percentage, fairway percentage, putts per hole, total putts, driving distance, total prize money, and total events played. The dataset consists of 158 players, each with unique playing styles and performance outcomes.

## Objective:
The central focus of this case study is to analyze whether or not the types of shots performed by players has on impact on the output. Does it matter how you get to your par? Is it sustainable to get up and down on every green? I want to analyze whether or not there is a viable strategy in terms of what shots to practice or avoid in order to win LPGA tournaments.

## Data Analysis:
This dataset included columns such as green regulation, percentage of par saves after being in the sand, fairway percentage, putts per hole, total putts, driving distance, total prize money, and total events played. First I conducted some scatter plots for total prize and average prize per round where we saw some correlations between variables. The couple variables that have a bit more of a trend would be green regulations which are positively correlated, sand percentage which is negatively correlated and putts per hole in which Lydia Ko (the player with highest earnings) had the lowest putts per hole average. I then created some boxplots where we saw low deviation for putts per round and high deviation among players for driving distance, green regulation and sand saves. I then created some histograms to get more of a feel for the distribution of players, this analysis showed clearly that total prize money heavily skewes to the right. This shows that in golf, as in many other professional sports, the earnings are not distributed very evenly with a couple very well payed athletes and the majority making much less. The last exploratory analysis I conducted was a heat map. When benchmarking Total Prize Money we see strong positive correlations for Events (0.62), GreenReg (0.45) and SandSaves (0.45). This is in agreement with our scatterplot analysis. We also saw negative correlation between AvePutts (-0.34) & TotPutts (-0.34) and Putts_per_hole (-0.34).
Next I created a couple of t-tests to see if there was performance differences between players with high earnings or wins from other players. I analyzed green regulation, fairway percentage, sand percentage, putts per hole, and driving distance. For each category I ran three t-tests splitting players with earning above $500K, $1M and at least 1 win for the 2022 season. Through this analysis I found that the only variable that wasn’t statistically significant between these groups was driving distance.
Next I conducted an Anova splitting players based on their earnings into 3 groups. I tested driving distance again and saw no significance across the three groups. I then tested Sand Percentage and saw statistical difference between high and low earners and also between medium and low earners but not between high and medium earners suggesting that improving sand performance may be a key factor in increasing earnings, particularly for lower-tier players, while for medium and high earners, other factors like greens in regulation or putting may play a more critical role in prize winnings. Lastly I conducted an Anova on putts per hole, which had surprising results. The ANOVA test confirms a statistically significant difference in putts per hole across earnings groups (p = 0.0000). High earners take significantly more putts per hole than low earners (p = 0.000), while medium earners also putt more than low earners (p = 0.000). However, there is no significant difference between high and medium earners (p = 0.3821). Surprisingly, low earners have the fewest putts per hole, contradicting the assumption that fewer putts lead to higher earnings. This suggests that higher earners may take more aggressive putts or rely more on birdie opportunities, whereas low earners may focus on conservative play and avoiding three-putts.
Lastly, I ran a couple regressions with Total prize money as my first Y variable, average prize money as my second and total wins as my last. Across the three OLS regression models, greens in regulation (greenReg) emerges as the most consistently significant predictor, positively impacting total prize money (p = 0.000), average prize per round (p = 0.000), and official wins (p = 0.002). Sand saves also show a positive effect on both total and average earnings (p = 0.015, p = 0.022) and slightly contribute to official wins (p = 0.041), while excessive sand play (sand percentage and sand attempts) negatively affects earnings. Total rounds (p = 0.039) and events played (p = 0.060, p = 0.078) show negative correlations with prize earnings, suggesting that more participation does not necessarily lead to higher per-round earnings. The two regressions comparing prize money have pretty good R² with totPrize at R² = 0.612 and average prize per round with an R² =  0.599, however, the model predicting official wins (R² = 0.263) is weak. This is most probably because we are not accounting for other placements and so 2nd place is not being acknowledged as very close to first place.

## Findings:
The shots most important to focus on to increase earnings and success in LPGA tournaments is hitting greens in regulation and saving par after being in a sand trap. Hitting fairways, putts per hole and driving distance do not differentiate winning players from the others. A lot of players think that hitting a long drive has a big impact on their game and that they are at a disadvantage if they are not very long hitters. That being said the data shows that distance is not a statistically significant variable in calculating a players success.
Options Considered:
Focus less on weight training to increase strength and driving distance
Spend more time practicing approach shots to hit greens in regulation
Spend time practicing getting up and down on greens from bunker positions

## Recommendations:
The insights derived from the data analysis, suggest that in order to maximize their chances of winning players should spend most of their efforts during practice on the shot to the green to get it in regulation and in the bunker to try and get up and down to save par.
